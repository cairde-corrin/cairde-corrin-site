# How to post news on the Cáirde Corrin website

This guide is for committee members who write news, add photos to the gallery, change
the website's pages, list articles about the group, or change the social
media posts on the home page. The same guide opens from the **Help** button in the
corner of every editor screen. You do not need to know anything about
websites to follow it. Keep it open the first few times; after that it takes a few
minutes.

## The short version

1. Go to the editor and log in.
2. Press **＋ news post**, fill in the boxes and press **Save**. Nothing is public yet.
3. When it is right, set **Status** to **Ready**, then **Publish** -> **Publish now**.
4. A few minutes later the post is on the website. If it is not there yet, see "After you
   publish" below before assuming anything went wrong.

## Getting in

The editor is at **https://cairde-corrin.netlify.app/admin/**. Once the website has its
own address it will be **https://www.cairdecorrin.ie/admin/**; both work after that. It
is not linked from the website, so bookmark it.

**The first time.** Someone who looks after the website sends you an invitation by email.
It comes from Netlify, the company that hosts the site. Press **Accept the invite** in
that email. The website opens and asks you to choose a password; that finishes it. From
then on you log in at the editor address with your email and that password.

**Logging in.** Open the editor, press **Login**, and enter your email and password.

**Forgotten password.** In the login window, press **Forgot password?** and follow the
email. If an invitation has expired or never arrived, ask for a new one; nobody can see
or reset your password for you.

## Writing a news post

Press **Contents** at the top, then **News** on the left, then **＋ news post**.

Each box has a short explanation under it. The ones that matter:

- **Headline.** Keep it short. The post's web address is made from the headline the
  first time you save, and stays the same afterwards even if you change the headline.
- **Date.** Already filled in with today's date. Change it only if the post should
  carry another date.
- **Written by.** Leave it as Cáirde Corrin, or put the name of the person who wrote it.
- **Short summary.** One or two sentences. This is what people see in the list of news
  and when the post is shared on social media.
- **The post.** The text itself. The buttons above it make text **bold** or *italic*, add
  a link, a heading, a quote or a list. Write as you would an email. Keep to the facts
  you know: if a number or a name is uncertain, leave it out or say it is uncertain.
- **Main photo**, **More photos** and **YouTube video** are all optional; see below.

Press **Save** at the top whenever you like. A saved post is a draft: only people who can
log in to the editor can see it.

## Photos

Use photos you took yourself, or ones you have permission to use. Do not post a photo in
which someone can be recognised unless they have agreed.

Photos straight from a phone are fine, however large. Before a photo is sent anywhere,
the editor makes a smaller copy of it on your own phone or computer, and only that copy
is uploaded. The copy keeps the picture and nothing else: **the location your phone
stored in the photo, and the time and the phone's details, never leave your device.**
Your original photo is not changed.

Add photos with the **Choose an image** button. Dragging a photo onto the page, or
pasting one in, is refused with a message, because that would skip this step.

**A main photo at the top of the post.** Under **Main photo (optional)**, press
**Choose an image**, then upload a photo from your computer or phone, or pick one you
uploaded before. Then fill in the box that asks you to describe the photo for people who
cannot see it: one plain sentence saying what is in it ("Volunteers pulling cherry laurel
beside the stream"). Do not start with "A photo of". A caption is optional.

**More photos at the end of the post.** Under **More photos (optional)**, press **Add
photo** once for each, and fill them in the same way.

**A photo in the middle of the text.** Click where you want it in **The post**, on a new
line. Press the **+** button at the right of the buttons above the text and choose
**Image**. Press **Choose an image**, upload or pick the photo, and fill in **Alt Text**
with the one-sentence description. Leave **Title** empty.

**On an iPhone,** Photo Library, Take Photo and Choose File all work. On a computer, an
iPhone photo in Apple's own format (HEIC) works in Safari; in Chrome or Firefox the
editor says it cannot read it, and nothing is uploaded. Upload a JPEG copy instead, or use
Safari.

## A YouTube video

Only for videos Cáirde Corrin made and put on its own YouTube channel.

On YouTube, open the video, press **Share**, copy the address, and paste it into the
**YouTube video (optional)** box. That is all.

On the website the post shows a still picture from the video with a play button. Nothing
is loaded from YouTube until a visitor presses play, which keeps the site's promise not
to share visitors' details with anyone just for opening a page.

**A video post works as soon as it is published, and needs nothing from anyone
afterwards.** You do not have to tell anybody or do anything else.

## Publishing

When the post is ready:

1. Press **Save** if you have changed anything since you last saved.
2. Press **Status** and choose **Ready**.
3. Press **Publish** and choose **Publish now**. Confirm when asked.

The website updates itself. Give it about two minutes, then open the News page and
refresh it. If the post is not there after five minutes, see "When something looks
wrong" below.

## After you publish

The website rebuilds itself after every publish, which takes a minute or two. After that,
your browser may still be showing the copy of the page it saved earlier: pages are kept
for up to five minutes. So if the change is not there:

1. Wait two minutes.
2. Reload the page with a **hard refresh**: on a computer, hold **Shift** and press the
   reload button (or press **Ctrl+Shift+R**, or **Cmd+Shift+R** on a Mac); on a phone,
   close the page and open it again.
3. If it is still not there after five minutes, see "When a change does not appear".

Every editor meets this the first time, and it almost never means publishing failed.

**Status** also offers **Draft** and **In review**. They change nothing on the website;
use them if you want someone else to read a post before it goes out. The **Workflow** tab
at the top shows every post that is not yet published, sorted into those three columns.
Click a post there to carry on with it.

## Changing or removing something already published

**To correct a post,** go to **Contents** -> **News**, open it, make the change, press
**Save**, then set **Status** to **Ready** and **Publish** -> **Publish now** again. The
website shows the old version until you publish the new one.

**To take a post off the website,** open it, tick **Take this off the website** at the
bottom, press **Save**, then publish it as usual. Once the site has rebuilt, the post has
no page and appears in no list. It stays in the editor, so you can put it back the same
way: untick, save, publish. Photos in the Gallery and Articles have the same tick. Use it
for anything published by mistake.

There is no button to delete a published post: the editor's own delete cannot work with
the protection that keeps the website safe. If something must go for good, for example a
photo someone has asked to have removed, take it off the website first, then ask the
person who looks after the website to delete it completely.

To throw away a draft that was never published, use **Delete unpublished entry**.

## Changing a page

Every page of the website is yours to change, apart from News, the gallery and Articles
(which have their own sections in this guide) and the Privacy and Terms pages.

The quickest way: while logged in to the editor, open the page on the website itself. A
green **Edit this page** button appears in the bottom corner (only you can see it, because
you are logged in); press it and the editor opens at that page.

Or press **Contents** -> **Pages** and choose the page. Each page has three parts:

- **About this page (for search engines and sharing).** Folded away at the top. The
  page's title and a one- or two-sentence summary: what search engines show, what appears
  on the browser tab and when someone shares the page. Some pages also say what they are
  about (the home page: the Glenabo catchment; the species page: the list's name).
  Search engines are told this whatever you do with the rest of the page, so keep it
  true to the page.
- **The top of the page.** The small line, the title and the opening paragraph; on a stop,
  its photo, position and height too. Every page has exactly one, always first, so it
  is not something you can add or remove.
- **Blocks (the rest of the page).** Everything else, in order, one block at a time.

### Working with blocks

- **Add a block:** press **Add block** at the foot of the list and choose the kind. It is
  added at the end; drag it to where you want it.
- **Move a block:** drag it by the handle on its left (or tab to the handle, press the
  space bar, use the arrow keys, and press the space bar again).
- **Remove a block:** press the cross on its right.
- **Copy a block:** open it, tick **Make a copy of this block when I save**, then save.
  The copy appears straight after it, ready to change; the tick clears itself.
- **Replace a block with a different kind:** add the new one, drag it into place and
  remove the old one.
- **Open or close a block:** press the arrow on its left. Closed, it shows its heading so
  you can find your way around a long page.

The kinds of block are the parts the website was already built from:

| Block | What it is |
|---|---|
| Text | A heading and paragraphs. On the home page, each Text block starts a new band across the page (light, dark or tinted), and the blocks after it sit in that band. |
| Figure box | Two to four figures side by side, such as "Catchment area: about 3.6 km²". |
| Single image | One photo, full width, with a caption. |
| Two photos side by side | Exactly two photos, each with a small line and a caption. |
| Photo carousel | A row of photos that scrolls sideways, with an optional link to the gallery. |
| Photo grid | Photos in a grid, each with a caption. |
| Numbered points | Short points with a number or label, such as the three strands of the work. |
| Cards | Boxes side by side, each with a title and text, and optionally a photo and a link. |
| List of links | Links to other websites, each with its name. |
| Note box | A boxed note, with an optional heading and link; it can be folded away until someone taps it. |
| Pull quote | A quotation set apart from the text. |
| Button link | A green button leading to another page. |
| YouTube video | A video that plays only when someone presses play. |
| Recording | One sound recording, with a heading and a line about it. |
| List of recordings, Films | The numbered recordings and the films on the Recordings page. |
| Folklore story | One story, with its source in the National Folklore Collection. |
| Species list | The species recorded, each with its name, photo, description and records. |
| Credits list | The entries on the Credits page. |
| Interactive map, List of stops, The walked loop | The parts of the Trails page; each can appear once. |
| Posts from the group | The home page's Instagram or social posts; once. |

**Writing text.** Type plain text. Leave an empty line between paragraphs. Put
`*asterisks*` round words to set them in italics. To make a link, write
`[the words](/trails/)` for a page on this site, or `[the words](https://...)` for
another website. In a heading, press Enter to start a new line.

**Figures are ordinary words.** "About 360 hectares" is typed like the rest of the
sentence, and you can change it. The same figure often appears on more than one page, so
if you change one, change the others too. Nothing stops you publishing, but if two pages
end up giving the same figure differently, the person who looks after the website is
warned and will check which is right. The same goes for counts: if the species page says
"13 species" and you add a fourteenth, change the 13.

**Photos.** Every photo field has three buttons:

- **Choose a photo already on the website** opens a list of every photo the website
  already has, with a search box: pick one to reuse it, rather than uploading it again
  (its description elsewhere on the site is shown, so you can copy it).
- **Upload a new photo** is for a photograph the group took, or one the photographer has
  given the group permission to publish. Never upload a picture found online: it almost
  certainly belongs to someone who has not agreed. Fill in **Who took this photo**: it
  says Cáirde Corrin to start with, which is right for the group's own; for anyone else's,
  put their name, and "Photo:" and their name appear under the photo.
- **Use a photo from Wikimedia Commons** is for a photo someone else took and shared on
  Wikimedia Commons, which is usually the best source for a plant or animal. Open the
  photo's own page on Commons (its address starts with
  `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:`), copy the address, paste it in and press
  **Check and use this photo**. The editor reads who took it and its licence from Commons
  there and then, and shows them. If Commons does not say clearly who took it, or the
  licence is not one the website can credit properly, the photo is refused and the reason
  shown: choose another. The photographer and licence appear under the photo on the
  website and on the Credits page automatically; you never type them.

Whichever you use, say in one sentence in **What the photo shows** what is in the photo. Some photos on the website, on the species and water pages,
are other people's, used under a licence that requires their name and the licence beneath
the photo. That credit is attached to the photo itself: wherever you put the photo, its
credit goes with it, and you cannot separate them. If you want to use a photo of someone
else's that is not on the website yet, ask the person who looks after the website to add
it with its credit.

**Folklore stories** must keep their source: the volume and page in the Schools'
Collection, where it was collected, and the link to it on dúchas.ie. The licence the
stories are used under requires it, so the website will not publish a story without it.

**The Credits page** keeps the credits a licence requires: those for other people's
photographs, the elevation data behind the maps, the species records and the folklore.
You can move them among the other entries, but not change them, and if one is removed it is
put back at the end of the page. Everything else on the Credits page is yours to change.

**The stops.** Stops 01 to 10 are all under **Pages**. A stop whose title is empty shows
"Not yet surveyed", and search engines are kept away from it. To bring a stop to life, fill
in the top of its page: the title, the photo and what it shows, the latitude and longitude,
the height in metres, the direction the photo faces, and when it was recorded; and its name
and summary under **About this page**. Publish, and the stop appears on the trails page, on
the map and in search engines. Take the position from the photo, not from memory.

**New routes on the trails page.** To add a route the group has walked, add a **Walked
route** block to the Trails page, give it a name, and upload the walk's GPS track: export
it from the phone app as a GPX file and press **Upload**. The website draws it on the map,
with its own elevation profile and a download. Before the file even leaves your computer,
the times and any names in it are removed; when the website is built, stretches walked
twice (out to a dead end and back, or past the start) are taken out so each appears once.
If the track cannot be used (it is not a GPX file, it has no heights in it, it is somewhere
else, or there is too little of it), the trails page is not changed and the person who looks
after the website is told why.

**The walked loop is not replaceable here.** The Glenabo loop, its elevation profile and
its download were cleaned from the original GPS track, checked against all three recorded
stops and checked by hand. Its block shows that work and has nothing to fill in. If the
loop ever needs replacing, that is a job for the person who looks after the website, not
an upload: add a new walk as a **Walked route** instead.

**Films** are added the way a news post's video is: put the film on the group's own
YouTube channel, then add a film to the **Films** block and paste its address (on
YouTube, press **Share** under the video and copy the address). The page shows a still
from the film with a play button, and nothing loads from YouTube until a visitor presses
play. Give it a name if you like, and the date it was filmed. The films already on the
page are kept on the website itself and have no address; leave them as they are.

**Recordings** use sound files already on the website. A new recording is added by the
person who looks after the website, because a phone's recording carries its location and
details, and they remove them first.

**Species.** Add only a species recorded in the Glenabo-Corrin catchment on one of the
group's own surveys: not one that is likely to be there, or was seen somewhere else. In
the description, say in your own words how it was identified (for example with Pl@ntNet,
and who confirmed it) and how confident the identification is, and under **Where and when
it was recorded** say where and when. A reference photo is usually best taken from
Wikimedia Commons, with **Use a photo from Wikimedia Commons**.

**If the editor will not save a page.** When you save, the editor checks the page the way
the website will. If something would stop the website updating (a photo with no
description, a "Two photos" block with one photo, a story with no source, a link that is not
a web address), it does not save, and tells you which block and what to fix. Nothing you
typed is lost: fix it and save again. See "When a change does not appear" below.

Save, set the status to Ready and publish, as for a post.

## Listing an article about the group

When a newspaper, magazine or another website writes about Cáirde Corrin or the
catchment, press **Contents** -> **Articles** -> **＋ article** and fill in its headline,
where it was published, who wrote it, its date and its address.

Then write **a summary in your own words**: two or three sentences saying what it is
about. Do not copy text from the article; it belongs to the publication. You may add **one
short line** quoted exactly from it, which the website shows in quotation marks with the
publication's name. Never more than that.

The Articles page, and its link in the menu, appear once the first article is published.
Each card links to the original.

## The gallery and the home page's social posts

**Gallery.** Press **Contents** -> **Gallery** -> **＋ photo**. Choose the photo, describe
it in one sentence, give it a caption, and if you know it, the date it was taken. Save,
set the status to Ready and publish, as for a post. The same rules about photos apply,
and the location is removed in the same way. The Gallery page and its link in the menu
appear once the first photo is published.

**Social media posts on the home page.** Press **Contents** -> **Home page** -> **Social
media posts**. You can list up to three Instagram or Facebook posts. For each, open the
post, use its **Share** button, **Copy link**, and paste the address in; then type one
short line saying what it is about. The home page shows them as cards that link to the
post. Nothing is loaded from Instagram or Facebook. Leave the list empty and the home
page simply has no social section. Save and publish as usual.

## What you cannot change here, and why

Some things are not in the editor at all, on purpose:

- **The privacy and terms pages,** which are the group's commitments to visitors: what the
  website collects, and what it promises about land access, accuracy and reuse. They are
  statements the group can be held to, not wording to edit.
- **The credits a licence requires,** for other people's photographs, the elevation data,
  the species records and the folklore (see "Changing a page" above). You can move them;
  they stay attached to what they credit.
- **The survey data:** the map, the catchment and action area, and the walked loop with its
  elevation profile and GPS download. The figures in the text you can change; the data
  they come from you cannot.

Each of these was checked against a source: a GPS track, an elevation survey, a licence. The website exists partly to be read
by landowners and people who know the ground, and those are what it will be judged on.
Changes to them go through the person who looks after the website, so that each change
can be checked against its source first. If you spot something wrong, or have something
to add, tell them.

## When a change does not appear

You published, and the website still shows the old version. There are three reasons, and
you can tell them apart.

**1. Your browser is showing the copy it saved earlier.** This is by far the most common.
The website takes a minute or two to update after you publish, and your browser keeps
pages for up to five minutes. Wait two minutes, then do a hard refresh (see "After you
publish"). If the change appears, that was all it was.

**2. The editor would not save the page.** You would have seen it at the time: when you
saved, a message said "This page has not been saved" and listed what to fix, in words such
as *Block 3 (Two photos side by side) needs exactly two photos, and it has 1.* The page at
the top of the editor still says **Unsaved changes**. Fix what the message says, and save
and publish again.

**3. The website could not update.** The editor checks everything it can before saving,
but a few things can only be checked when the website is built: whether an uploaded photo
can be read, or whether a GPS track can be used. If one of those fails, the website does
not update at all and stays exactly as it was, so nothing is broken or half-changed. You
can tell it is this when the editor said **Changes saved** and the post or page is no
longer in the **Workflow** tab, but ten minutes and a hard refresh later the website still
shows the old version.

The person who looks after the website is sent an email about it, but you are the one
who knows what you just changed, and you may notice first. So when it happens:

1. **Do not publish the same change again.** It will stop in the same place each time.
   Nothing else you or anyone else publishes will appear either until it is fixed.
2. **Think about what you added that the editor could not check: a new photo you
   uploaded, or a GPS track.** That is almost always the cause. If you can, open the page,
   remove that photo or that Walked route block (or choose a photo already on the
   website instead), save and publish. If the website then updates, that file was the
   problem: keep it aside and tell the person who looks after the website.
3. **Tell the person who looks after the website,** the same day, whether or not step 2
   worked. Say which page you changed, roughly what you changed, and the time you
   published. They can read the reason the website gives, in plain words naming the page
   and the block, and will tell you what to fix or fix it themselves.

**Who to contact.** For 1, nobody: it sorts itself out. For 2, nobody: the message tells you
what to fix. For 3, or whenever you are not sure, the person who looks after the website,
as above.

## When something looks wrong

- **Publish says it failed, straight after you saved.** The website checks every change
  before it can be published, and that takes a minute. Wait a minute and press Publish
  again.
- **The post is not on the website after five minutes.** Do a hard refresh (see "After
  you publish"). Then look in the **Workflow** tab: if the post is still there, it has not
  been published; set its status to **Ready** and publish it. If it is not there but still
  is not on the website, see "When a change does not appear" above.
- **A photo will not upload.** If the editor says it cannot read it, it is probably an
  iPhone photo in Apple's own format opened in Chrome or Firefox (see "On an iPhone"
  above). If you dragged it onto the page, use **Choose an image** instead.
- **The video shows a play button but no picture.** YouTube could not be reached at the
  moment the website updated. The video still plays. The picture appears by itself the
  next time anything is published.
- **You published something by mistake, or with a mistake in it.** Correct it and publish
  again, or delete it. If it is urgent and you cannot fix it, tell the person who looks
  after the website: they can put the whole site back to how it was within a minute.
- **You cannot log in.** Use **Forgot password?**. If that email never comes, ask for a
  new invitation.
- **The invitation or password link opened the website's home page instead of the
  editor.** Wait a moment; it should move on to the editor by itself. If it does not, go
  to the editor address yourself and try **Forgot password?**.
- **Two people editing the same post at once** can overwrite each other's changes. Agree
  who is doing which post.

Nothing you do in the editor can damage the trails, stops, species or any other page,
and anything published can be corrected. If in doubt, save it as a draft and ask.
